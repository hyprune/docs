Work on Hyprune started on **7 October 2026**. As of 10 October, core, shell, SDK, schemas and two worlds run together end to end, the IPC protocol is at version 0.16 and the release world has shipped ten versions. This page records how it got there, with the evidence kept in each repository. The [roadmap](/roadmap/) says what is still missing.

All images and clips on this page are in-engine captures of **Lumen Reach**, the official release world (Hyprune contributors, CC-BY-4.0).

## Frame rate, power and memory

The test machine is a laptop with an Intel Panther Lake iGPU driving a 4K output, often on USB-C power. Every number below comes from an isolated nested session with Lumen Reach unless noted. The source documents are in the core repository.

### From about 15 to 60 frames per second at 4K

At native 4K with fixed full-resolution rendering, the median frame interval was **66.6 ms (about 15 FPS)**. Dynamic resolution brought it to a **16.7 ms median (60 FPS)** on 8 October. Most of the gain came from rendering fewer pixels and cutting memory traffic, not from drawing fewer objects. A later run sustained **60.01 newly rendered frames per second for 23 seconds with zero reused frames**, at automatic Low quality and a world render scale of 0.375–0.5.

### Render on demand (9 October)

A world frame is rendered only when its inputs change: the camera, a window's content or an animation. When nothing changes, the last frame is presented again.

<div class="stats">
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">World frames, static scene</span><span class="stat-value">0 /s</span><span class="stat-delta">from 59.7 /s</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Compositor GPU busy, static</span><span class="stat-value">1.2%</span><span class="stat-delta">from 42.1%</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">GPU idle residency</span><span class="stat-value">88.0%</span><span class="stat-delta">from 43.5%, system-wide</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">GPU memory estimate</span><span class="stat-value">269.5 MiB</span><span class="stat-delta">from 373.6 MiB, same scene</span></div>
</div>

A window updating at about 30 Hz now costs 28.9 world frames per second instead of 60. The first frame after idle took 32 ms. The memory saving comes from one colour upload, a uniform buffer, frustum culling and indexed draws.

On 10 October idle got cheaper again. Workspace walls had been recomposed on every tick even when nothing on them changed, and the default workspace homes made most area walls into such composites, so idle cost had crept up to about 2%. Walls now recompose only when their windows, popups or stacking order change:

<div class="stats">
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Nested GPU busy, at rest</span><span class="stat-value">0.07%</span><span class="stat-delta">from 1.5–2.0%</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Whole session, at rest</span><span class="stat-value">0.08%</span><span class="stat-delta">from 2.17%</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">With a ~30 Hz client</span><span class="stat-value">44%</span><span class="stat-delta">from 52% GPU busy</span></div>
</div>

A wall showing a live app still updates as it changes. Walls also draw windows in Hyprland's own stacking order now, so raising a window shows on the wall too.

### FSR 1 upscaling (9 October)

The world renders below output resolution and is upscaled. AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution 1 (EASU + RCAS) is now the default upscaler, with bilinear plus sharpening as the fallback; since [IPC 0.16](/reference/schema/ipc/0.16/) the shell's Graphics settings can choose either. It costs about 2 ms of GPU time per frame at 0.375–0.5 scale, which dynamic resolution absorbs. It keeps edges and screen text crisper:

<Shot name="fsr1-vs-bilinear-stairs" alt="Two crops of the same stairs and railing at 0.375 render scale. Left: bilinear upscaling with blocky edges. Right: FSR 1 with cleaner edges." caption="Arrival stairs at 0.375 render scale: bilinear plus sharpen (left), FSR 1 (right)." version="0.7.0" />

### Compressed assets: Lumen Reach 0.8.0 (10 October)

World format 0.7 (9 October) lets packages use KTX2/Basis textures, meshopt compression and quantised meshes; lightmaps stay PNG. Lumen Reach 0.8.0 is the first release built this way. The optimiser is deterministic and a verifier checks that every manifest index, collision and UV layout survives. Measured against 0.7.2 in the same nested session (4K, Intel, Medium, fixed 0.5 scale):

<div class="stats">
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Package</span><span class="stat-value">41.4 MiB</span><span class="stat-delta">from 70.5 MiB (−41%)</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">GPU memory</span><span class="stat-value">149.8 MiB</span><span class="stat-delta">from 270.5 MiB (−45%)</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Load to ready (warm)</span><span class="stat-value">2.06 s</span><span class="stat-delta">from 2.68 s (−23%)</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">GPU frame time</span><span class="stat-value">9.40 ms</span><span class="stat-delta">from 9.43 ms (unchanged)</span></div>
</div>

The 4K Intel frame is limited by fill rate, so compression saves memory, load time and download size, not frame time. The images stay close: 36.2–41.3 dB PSNR and a mean CIEDE2000 difference of 0.53–0.87 against 0.7.2. The release build now includes a **performance gate**. It renders 400 poses along every route edge against the previous release and fails if median GPU time grows more than 8% or p95 more than 12%. 0.8.0 passed at +0.4% / −0.4%.

### Shader work (10 October)

<div class="stats">
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">World shading, Intel Low</span><span class="stat-value">1.66 ms</span><span class="stat-delta">from 2.11 ms (−21%)</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Shader instructions, Low</span><span class="stat-value">316</span><span class="stat-delta">from 677</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Longest upload job, cold</span><span class="stat-value">31 ms</span><span class="stat-delta">from 51 ms</span></div>
</div>

The world shader is now compiled per effect preset, so features a preset turns off are removed at compile time instead of being skipped at run time. The stills are identical or within ±5 on a few dozen of 8.3 million pixels. Shaders also compile in parallel with the asset upload (`KHR_parallel_shader_compile`), so first loads stall less. Medium and above should gain less; they weren't measured. Two other ideas were measured and dropped because they didn't help on Intel: sorting opaque draws front to back, and blitting depth in the upscale pass.

### Derived visibility (10 October)

A new tool derives visibility regions from the navmesh and the scene, instead of authoring them by hand. On Lumen Reach it produced 160 regions in about 51 seconds. Across 64 test views and 5 targeted probes it changed no pixels, and it culled more than the hand-written regions (mean batches submitted 44.7 → 40.5). Lumen Reach 0.9.0 runs it as a build step: 805 cells merge into 184 regions, and none of 29,781 eye samples hides anything it should see. Across the same 72 test views as 0.8.1, batches submitted dropped from 44.2 to 40.4, and culled frames matched unculled ones in 72 of 72 views (0.8.1: 71 of 72).

### Optional dGPU offload (RFC-0009, 8 October)

On laptops with a discrete GPU, a supervised Vulkan worker can render the world there and hand frames back over DMA-BUF. It runs under a power budget: 50 W / 45 FPS on USB-C or battery, 80 W / 60 FPS on qualified mains power. Phase 1 is merged and **off by default**. Performance → Offload switches it at runtime, and the renderer falls back to local rendering immediately if the worker stalls. At native 4K on USB-C the worker held 45 FPS at a 32.8 W peak.

<Video name="dgpu-quality" title="Intel Auto compared with NVIDIA Ultra at 4K" width={1280} height={498} caption="Prototype quality comparison at 4K, Sun Court and Rime Vault: Intel Auto (Low, dynamic scale) against an NVIDIA laptop GPU at native 4K with 4× MSAA, capped at 45 FPS. World only; no application windows." />

The Intel iGPU already reaches 60 FPS at 4K with dynamic resolution, so offload is about quality headroom, not reaching 60. It has not been qualified on physical power profiles yet.
