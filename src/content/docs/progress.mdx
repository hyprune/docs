Work on Hyprune started on **7 October 2026**. As of 13 October, core, shell, SDK, schemas, two worlds and the first object library run together end to end. The IPC protocol is at version 0.24, the world format at 0.9, and the release world has shipped seventeen versions. This page records how it got there, with the evidence kept in each repository. The [roadmap](/roadmap/) says what is still missing.

All images and clips on this page are in-engine captures of **Lumen Reach**, the official release world (Hyprune contributors, CC-BY-4.0), except the reticle clip, which uses core's original nested test world.

## Frame rate, power and memory

The test machine is a laptop with an Intel Panther Lake iGPU driving a 4K output, often on USB-C power. Every number below comes from an isolated nested session with Lumen Reach unless noted. The source documents are in the core repository.

### From about 15 to 60 frames per second at 4K

At native 4K with fixed full-resolution rendering, the median frame interval was **66.6 ms (about 15 FPS)**. Dynamic resolution brought it to a **16.7 ms median (60 FPS)** on 8 October. Most of the gain came from rendering fewer pixels and cutting memory traffic, not from drawing fewer objects. A later run sustained **60.01 newly rendered frames per second for 23 seconds with zero reused frames**, at automatic Low quality and a world render scale of 0.375–0.5.

### Render on demand (9 October)

A world frame is rendered only when its inputs change: the camera, a window's content or an animation. When nothing changes, the last frame is presented again.

A window updating at about 30 Hz now costs 28.9 world frames per second instead of 60. The first frame after idle took 32 ms. The memory saving comes from one colour upload, a uniform buffer, frustum culling and indexed draws.

On 10 October idle got cheaper again. Workspace walls had been recomposed on every tick even when nothing on them changed, and the default workspace homes made most area walls into such composites, so idle cost had crept up to about 2%. Walls now recompose only when their windows, popups or stacking order change:

A wall showing a live app still updates as it changes. Walls also draw windows in Hyprland's own stacking order now, so raising a window shows on the wall too.

### FSR 1 upscaling (9 October)

The world renders below output resolution and is upscaled. AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution 1 (EASU + RCAS) is now the default upscaler, with bilinear plus sharpening as the fallback; since [IPC 0.16](/reference/schema/ipc/0.16/) the shell's Graphics settings can choose either. It costs about 2 ms of GPU time per frame at 0.375–0.5 scale, which dynamic resolution absorbs. It keeps edges and screen text crisper:

### Compressed assets: Lumen Reach 0.8.0 (10 October)

World format 0.7 (9 October) lets packages use KTX2/Basis textures, meshopt compression and quantised meshes; lightmaps stay PNG. Lumen Reach 0.8.0 is the first release built this way. The optimiser is deterministic and a verifier checks that every manifest index, collision and UV layout survives. Measured against 0.7.2 in the same nested session (4K, Intel, Medium, fixed 0.5 scale):

  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Package</span><span class="stat-value">41.4 MiB</span><span class="stat-delta">from 70.5 MiB (−41%)</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">GPU memory</span><span class="stat-value">149.8 MiB</span><span class="stat-delta">from 270.5 MiB (−45%)</span></div>

The 4K Intel frame is limited by fill rate, so compression saves memory, load time and download size, not frame time. The images stay close: 36.2–41.3 dB PSNR and a mean CIEDE2000 difference of 0.53–0.87 against 0.7.2. The release build now includes a **performance gate**. It renders 400 poses along every route edge against the previous release and fails if median GPU time grows more than 8% or p95 more than 12%. 0.8.0 passed at +0.4% / −0.4%.

### Shader work (10 October)

<div class="stats">
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">World shading, Intel Low</span><span class="stat-value">1.66 ms</span><span class="stat-delta">from 2.11 ms (−21%)</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Shader instructions, Low</span><span class="stat-value">316</span><span class="stat-delta">from 677</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Longest upload job, cold</span><span class="stat-value">31 ms</span><span class="stat-delta">from 51 ms</span></div>
</div>

The world shader is now compiled per effect preset, so features a preset turns off are removed at compile time instead of being skipped at run time. The stills are identical or within ±5 on a few dozen of 8.3 million pixels. Shaders also compile in parallel with the asset upload (`KHR_parallel_shader_compile`), so first loads stall less. Medium and above should gain less; they weren't measured. Two other ideas were measured and dropped because they didn't help on Intel: sorting opaque draws front to back, and blitting depth in the upscale pass.

### Derived visibility (10 October)

A new tool derives visibility regions from the navmesh and the scene, instead of authoring them by hand. On Lumen Reach it produced 160 regions in about 51 seconds. Across 64 test views and 5 targeted probes it changed no pixels, and it culled more than the hand-written regions (mean batches submitted 44.7 → 40.5). Lumen Reach 0.9.0 runs it as a build step: 805 cells merge into 184 visibility areas, and none of 29,781 eye samples hides anything it should see. Across the same 72 test views as 0.8.1, batches submitted dropped from 44.2 to 40.4, and culled frames matched unculled ones in 72 of 72 views (0.8.1: 71 of 72).

### Motion feel and clarity (10 October)

Two things made moving around feel heavier than the frame rate suggested. The simulation ticked on its own 16 ms timer, which beats against the 60 Hz display, so a shown frame moved by zero, one or two steps. And when the GPU fell behind, new and repeated frames mixed at random. Now a tick runs on each presented frame, and movement and look use that frame's real time. A frame-rate target below the display rate renders on every second (or nth) presentation, evenly. Measured in nested 4K on the Intel iGPU, with the same scripted walk and turn before and after (lower variation is smoother):

  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Turning unevenness, default</span><span class="stat-value">0.022</span><span class="stat-delta">from 0.182</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Frame interval variation, GPU-bound</span><span class="stat-value">0.002</span><span class="stat-delta">from 0.339, now a steady 30</span></div>
  <div class="stat"><span class="stat-label">Wall text sharpness vs native</span><span class="stat-value">0.889</span><span class="stat-delta">from 0.477</span></div>

Input to first new frame stays at 8–10 ms at the default 60 FPS. A steady 30 FPS target adds one frame (22–29 ms). Two approaches were tried and dropped: interpolating between fixed steps, which made walking worse, and automatic slowdowns, which let the GPU clock down and never recover.

Workspace walls used to be composed into a fixed 1280×720 texture, which a large wall magnified almost three times at a normal viewing distance. Walls with windows are now composed at the monitor's own resolution with mipmaps, and sampled with trilinear and anisotropic filtering. They are regenerated only when the windows change, and cost about 44 MB per wall at 4K.

<figure class="shot"><img src="/media/core/wall-text-clarity.webp" width="1820" height="240" loading="lazy" alt="Three crops of the same terminal text: crisp native text, a blurry wall from before, and a crisp wall from after." /><figcaption>The same terminal text at 8 m: native (left), wall before (middle), wall after (right).</figcaption></figure>

Starting a carry on a window seen from behind used to stall the compositor for about 239 ms, because the turn-around sweep tested every world triangle at every step. With a broad phase and the world's collision index, it now takes 0.16 ms. Other small changes the same day: world geometry is static to gestures and tools; a window carried from behind turns to face you; empty walls show the wallpaper or a placeholder; placed windows snap into tool-mount frames; and render-pass tracking recovers when the compositor skips the end of a pass.

### View bob and the reticle (10 October)

[IPC 0.19](/reference/schema/ipc/0.19/) adds player settings for field of view (default 65°), walk and run speed (4 and 10 m/s), reach limits and the reticle. View bob is subtle by default: one smooth 1.0 cm dip per footstep at a walking 1.9 Hz, cut to 30% at 2.5 Hz when running. It never rises above standing height, so it can't poke through a low ceiling. It is off in flight, noclip, climbing, typing and scripted driving, and always off with reduced motion. It is view-only, so picking, the reticle and recordings never see it. Measured at 4 m/s, the dip peaks at 0.68–0.98 cm and settles to exactly zero within 1.6 s of stopping.

The reticle has six styles. It opens up while you move and shows what the current tool would do: valid, out of reach (with the distance) or invalid. Every state changes shape as well as colour.

### Fixes from testing (10–11 October)

Most of 10 October's work came from walking the release world on real hardware and replaying what testers captured:

- World geometry is static: tools and gestures can no longer nudge a building.
- A window can be carried from behind; it turns to face you. Starting that carry stalled the compositor for 234–239 ms because the turn-around sweep tested every triangle at every step. A broad phase and the world's collision index bring it to 0.15–0.17 ms.
- An empty workspace wall shows your wallpaper, or a Hyprune placeholder, instead of a black panel.
- A window released over a tool mount snaps into its frame, which highlights first.
- Windows that already exist when a world loads take their authored docks instead of waiting in the pocket.
- A tester's capture showed black specks on a roughness-1 surface on Intel. Replaying the capture pose in a nested session traced them to invalid values in the world shader. With guards in place, black pixels at that pose dropped from 7,800 to 5.

Captures got notes too ([IPC 0.17](/reference/schema/ipc/0.17/)). Press U in the world and the shell asks **why?** The note is saved with the capture and collected in one file, so a test walk turns into a readable list of findings.

<Shot name="capture-note-why-prompt-sheet" alt="Two crops of the HUD showing a WHY? note prompt after a capture, in the night and day themes, with example notes about a rail and a missing handrail." caption="The note prompt after U, in the Night and Day themes." version="0.9.0" />

A second, headless test machine with two GPUs now runs isolated nested sessions, and every night it runs every automatable suite, the schema, SDK and shell tests and the world builds. In its first day it found a render-pass tracking leak (a pass that starts and never ends after a failed frame), a daemon start-up race, GL errors wrongly blamed on world uploads, and offload refusing NVIDIA compositors and desktops without a battery. All are fixed.

### Optional dGPU offload (RFC-0009, 8 October)

On laptops with a discrete GPU, a supervised Vulkan worker can render the world there and hand frames back over DMA-BUF. It runs under a power budget: 50 W / 45 FPS on USB-C or battery, 80 W / 60 FPS on qualified mains power. Phase 1 is merged and **off by default**. Performance → Offload switches it at runtime, and the renderer falls back to local rendering immediately if the worker stalls. At native 4K on USB-C the worker held 45 FPS at a 32.8 W peak.

<Video name="dgpu-quality" title="Intel Auto compared with NVIDIA Ultra at 4K" width={1280} height={498} caption="Prototype quality comparison at 4K, Sun Court and Rime Vault: Intel Auto (Low, dynamic scale) against an NVIDIA laptop GPU at native 4K with 4× MSAA, capped at 45 FPS. World only; no application windows." />

The Intel iGPU already reaches 60 FPS at 4K with dynamic resolution, so offload is about quality headroom, not reaching 60. It has not been qualified on physical power profiles yet.

On 10 October offload also started working when the compositor itself runs on an NVIDIA GPU. A desktop with two RTX 3090s was tested at 1080p and 4K, both same-GPU and cross-GPU. In all five configurations offload stayed active at 60 FPS. [IPC 0.18](/reference/schema/ipc/0.18/) picks the offload GPU: by default an NVIDIA GPU that isn't driving the display, then the least busy. It also scales the power cap to that GPU. Without a battery the cap is 75% of the GPU's enforced power limit; laptops keep the 50 W / 80 W profile ceilings.
